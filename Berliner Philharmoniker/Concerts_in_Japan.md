# Berliner Philharmoniker — Concerts in Japan

A chronological record of all confirmed Berliner Philharmoniker tours and concerts in Japan (1957–2025). 25 tours total.

---

**Organizer:** NHK

| 11 Nov | Yahata | Yahata Seitetsu Taiikukan (八幡製鉄体育館) | — |
| 13 Nov | Hiroshima | Hiroshima Shimin Kōkaidō (広島市公会堂) | — |
| 15–16 Nov | Takarazuka | Takarazuka Daigekijō (宝塚大劇場) | — |
| 17 Nov | Kobe | Kōkusai Kaikan (神戸国際会館) | — |
| 19–20 Nov | Tokyo | Hibiya Kōkaidō / Sankei Hall | — |
| 21 Nov | Sendai | Sendai Shimin Kōkaidō (仙台市公会堂) | Conducted by Wilhelm Schüchter |

**Soloists:** None documented

**Audience reception:** The orchestra arrived at Haneda Airport on 31 October to a hero's welcome. NHK broadcast the opening concert of 3 November live in stereo on radio and television — millions tuned in. Long queues formed outside every venue. Considered a landmark event in Japanese classical music history.

## Tour 9 — April–May 1988

**Conductor:** Herbert von Karajan  
**No. of concerts:** 5  
**Context:** Karajan's **final Japan tour** and final tour anywhere; he died on 16 July 1989. First BPO concerts at Suntory Hall (opened 1986; Karajan had consulted on its acoustic design).

| Date | City | Venue | Program |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| 29 Apr | Osaka | Festival Hall | Mozart: Sinfonie Nr. 29 A-Dur KV 201 · Tschaikowsky: Sinfonie Nr. 6 h-Moll op. 74 (*Pathétique*) |
| 30 Apr | Osaka | Festival Hall | Beethoven: Sinfonie Nr. 4 B-Dur · Mussorgsky/Ravel: *Bilder einer Ausstellung* |
| 2 May | Tokyo | Suntory Hall (サントリーホール) | Mozart: Sinfonie Nr. 29 · Tschaikowsky: Sinfonie Nr. 6 (*Pathétique*) |
| 4 May | Tokyo | Tokyo Bunka Kaikan | Beethoven: Sinfonie Nr. 4 · Mussorgsky/Ravel: *Bilder einer Ausstellung* |
| 5 May | Tokyo | Suntory Hall | Mozart: Sinfonie Nr. 39 Es-Dur KV 543 · Brahms: Sinfonie Nr. 1 c-Moll op. 68 |

**Soloists:** None — all-orchestral programs

**Audience reception:** Sold-out houses; an emotional atmosphere given Karajan's visible decline. The 5 May Suntory Hall performance (Mozart / Brahms Nr. 1) was released on CD and is regarded as one of his most powerful late recordings — intense and deeply personal. The *Pathétique* and *Bilder* performances were also later issued.

## Tour 18 — November 2011

| Program | Dates | Venue | Content |
|---------|-------|-------|---------|
| **A** | 22, 24 Nov | Tokyo, Suntory Hall | Mahler: Sinfonie Nr. 9 D-Dur |

**Soloists (Program B):** Stefan Dohr (Horn; Hosokawa *Blossoming II* — world premiere context)

**Audience reception:** The return to Japan just eight months after the disaster carried deep emotional significance. Mahler 9 performances were greeted with extended silence before applause broke out.

| Program | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **A** | Mozart: Sinfonie Nr. 29 A-Dur KV 201 · Berg: Drei Orchesterstücke op. 6 · Brahms: Sinfonie Nr. 4 e-Moll op. 98 |
| **B** | Reger: *Mozart-Variationen* op. 132 · Strauss: *Ein Heldenleben* op. 40 |

**Soloists:** Daishin Kashimoto (Violine; solo role in *Ein Heldenleben* as BPO concertmaster)

**Special events:**
- **26 November** — Final Suntory Hall concert attended by Emperor Naruhito (天覧公演); Emperor met privately with Petrenko, General Manager Zietzschmann, and concertmasters backstage
- **"Be Phil Orchestra Japan"** — BPO members performed alongside 98 Japanese amateur musicians under Petrenko and Raphael Haeger: Brahms: Doppelkonzert a-Moll op. 102; excerpts from Prokofiev *Romeo und Julia*

**Audience reception:** Described in the press as "highly acclaimed." The post-COVID return was emotionally significant for both the orchestra and Japanese audiences.
