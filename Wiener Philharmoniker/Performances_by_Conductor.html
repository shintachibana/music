Programme tendencies — conductors with more than ten concerts

Repertoire patterns across each conductor's documented Japan tours with the orchestra. WPO is self-governing — there is no Chefdirigent tradition — so the dozen below are simply the long-form guest collaborators.

Riccardo Muti · 38 concerts, 115 performances

Schubert 29 · Mozart 16 · Rossini 9 · J. Strauss II 8 · Verdi 8

Long-standing WPO touring partner. Schubert is the spine — four different symphonies appear, with the Große C-major as the tour signature (8 performances). Rossini overtures, particularly Semiramide (9), and Strawinsky's Le baiser de la fée Divertimento (6) recur as house pieces. Italian lyricism met Schubertian breadth.

Lorin Maazel · 29 concerts, 95 performances

Beethoven 25 · J. Strauss II 20 · Mozart 10 · R. Strauss 10 · Brahms 7

Pure canon-virtuoso. Beethoven 5 + 6, Mozart 25, R. Strauss Don Juan, and Tschaikowsky 5 each appear seven times — a tight rotation of canon symphonies framed by a tone-poem closer. Strauss-family encores recur after the symphonic main, Maazel's signature New-Year-style finish on tour.

Paul Hindemith · 17 concerts, 78 performances

Mozart 28 · Beethoven 12 · Bach 7 · Wagner 7 · Brahms 6

The orchestra's 1956 maiden Japan tour, led almost entirely by Hindemith as both composer-conductor and ambassador. A panorama of German tradition: Bach Suite 2, Mozart's Fagottkonzert and 3rd Horn Concerto, Wagner's Siegfried-Idyll, Brahms Haydn-Variationen, Beethoven 4 — pedagogical curation with a single Hindemith piece (the Sinfonietta) per evening.

Valery Gergiev · 18 concerts, 64 performances

J. Strauss II 25 · Tschaikowsky 18 · Prokofjew 8 · Debussy 4 · Strawinsky 3

Two tours (2004, 2020). Tschaikowsky 6 Pathétique (9 performances) is the spine. A J. Strauss medley — Krönungslieder, Niko-Polka, Kaiser-Walzer, Persischer Marsch, Wiener Blut (5 each in 2004) — paired with Prokofjew Piano Concerto 2 (Matsuev) for a Russian / Vienna pairing characteristic of his approach.

Zubin Mehta · 21 concerts, 60 performances

R. Strauss 9 · Debussy 8 · Mozart 8 · Webern 6 · Brahms 5

Tone-poem virtuoso. Debussy La mer (6) and R. Strauss Ein Heldenleben (5) are his calling cards. Brahms concertos with Buchbinder and Bronfman, Bruckner 7 and 8, and Webern's Sechs Stücke op. 6 sit comfortably next to the late-Romantic core.

Claudio Abbado · 30 concerts, 59 performances

Beethoven 27 · Mozart 10 · Rossini 5 · Schubert 4 · Webern 4

Beethoven Eroica (8) is the anchor, with Mozart 40 (5) and a Rossini Il viaggio a Reims staged-opera run (5) carrying the Italian operatic side. Webern's Fünf Stücke op. 10 (4) is the modernist marker — a hint of the same Berg / Webern thread Abbado pursued at the BPO.

Seiji Ozawa · 19 concerts, 50 performances

Brahms 17 · Mozart 7 · Dvořák 5 · Bartók 4 · Haydn 4

Brahms-centred. A near-complete Brahms cycle (1 and 4 with 5 and 4 performances, plus 2 and 3 multiple times), then Bartók Wonderful Mandarin Suite (4), Dvořák New World (4) and Haydn 60 Il distratto (4) round it out. A global-citizen mix with the Romantic German core kept warm.

Christian Thielemann · 22 concerts, 49 performances

Beethoven 18 · R. Strauss 10 · Brahms 5 · Bruckner 5 · J. Strauss II 5

Late-Romantic German weight. Brahms 4 and Schumann Rheinische (4 each) lead, with Bruckner 5 (Nowak) and 8 (Haas) sitting deep in the catalogue. Beethoven Pastorale (3) and Strauss orchestral songs with Hampson complete the picture — a direct continuator of the Karajan / Böhm line at WPO.

Georg Solti · 17 concerts, 46 performances

Beethoven 10 · R. Strauss 10 · Schubert 9 · Strawinsky 3 · Tschaikowsky 3

Tight Beethoven-Strauss-Schubert core: Beethoven 7 (10) and R. Strauss Till Eulenspiegels lustige Streiche (10) repeated obsessively, plus Schubert Unvollendete (8). Solti's WPO tours were canon-anchored power-stage evenings — very few outliers.

Karl Böhm · 14 concerts, 39 performances

Beethoven 16 · J. Strauss II 6 · Brahms 4 · Schubert 4 · Mozart 3

Mozart and Schubert specialist. Beethoven 5, 6, and 7 (3 each), Schubert Unvollendete and Große (2 each), Mozart 29 and Jupiter. Strauss-family encores after the symphonic main. Pure Viennese house repertoire.

Andris Nelsons · 12 concerts, 31 performances

Dvořák 7 · Beethoven 3 · Haydn 3 · Mozart 3 · Mussorgsky 3

Newer-generation eclectic. Dvořák New World (4), Beethoven Piano Concerto 3 (3), Haydn 103 Paukenwirbel (3), Mozart 33 (3), Mussorgsky Khovanshchina Prelude (3), R. Strauss Ein Heldenleben (3), Shostakovich 9 (3). Short repertoire span but eclectic, including Henri Tomasi's Trombone Concerto — almost unique to him in our data.

Christoph Eschenbach · 11 concerts, 30 performances

Mozart 12 · Liszt 4 · Bruckner 3 · Prokofjew 2 · Schubert 2

Liszt Piano Concerto 1 (4 performances) — a piano-conductor's signature, rarely toured by anyone else — plus Bruckner 4 Romantische (3) and Mozart Jupiter (3). A distinctive Liszt / Brucknerian fingerprint.
